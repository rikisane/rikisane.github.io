## Lifelong Learning: What exactly is it?

Lifelong learning is any learning that pertains to improving your knowledge, skills, and competences, within one’s personal and professional lives [1]. It is generally voluntary, and involves self-motivation, extending beyond traditional education in order to enhance skills and satisfy one’s personal fulfillment . This is a crucial habit to maintain throughout a career, as it expands experiences, strengthens problem solving skills, forms new connections, keeps up with the curve, and allows an individual to continuously grow throughout their career. 


## Pursuing Further Nontraditional Education

One of the most common approaches to lifelong learning is to continue education past a traditional bachelor’s degree. According to a salary survey conducted in 2025 by the National Association of Colleges and Employers, engineers with a bachelor’s degree earned an average salary of around $80,482, while those with a master’s degree earned an average of $101,544 [2]. Not only does this benefit financial stability in the future, it also expands opportunities for jobs, research opportunities, leadership roles, and overall helps to strengthen an engineer’s skills. Going even further into something like a PhD can provide significant experience to an engineer, as it requires significant research into a desired topic. Although I have not finished my bachelor's degree yet, pursuing a master’s degree has always been a likely option for me in the future, and can be achieved with further research into University of Hawaii’s master’s program. 

## Getting certifications

Another beneficial component of lifelong learning is obtaining certifications past your traditional degree. Things like getting a PE (professional engineering) or CCIE (Cisco Certified Internetwork Expert) can substantially increase salaries, opportunities, and growth of knowledge. According to the University of San Diego: Professional and Continuing Education, these offer verified expertise, propelling one’s career forward and further placing commitments in fields of interest [3]. These also verify that you are compliant with licensing boards, as the engineering profession continues to evolve quickly with time. I have already started to study for my CCIE certificate, and will hope to achieve this before I graduate so that I have experience going into the field. 

## Mentorships

Getting mentorships throughout your journey is one of the most important and beneficial methods of lifelong learning. As noted by CED Engineering, a mentorship “offers a wide array of benefits that contribute significantly to the personal and professional growth of both mentors and mentees” [4]. Not only does this provide technical skill advancement, but also live feedback and practical experience from someone who has been in your shoes before. It can open doors for networking, career guidance, and gives a mentee that “confidence boost” they might need. I have had a mentorship during an AI programmer internship, and I found it highly beneficial as it was a direct resource to ask for questions and feedback to improve my product and overall mindset. 

## Participating In Organizations

Participating in any organization, related or not related to your field is a very important part of being successful in your career. According to the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE), being a member opens the door to “over 425,000 technology and engineering professionals united by a common desire to continuously learn, interact, collaborate, and innovate” [5]. This can provide lifelong relationships with those who have similar ambitions as you, and allows you to surround yourself with valuable ideas, networks, etc. Although I have yet to join IEEE, I am planning to join by registering on their website, and selecting relevant technical societies that align with my career interests. 

## Attending conferences or symposiums 

Being in the forefront or even the audience of conferences can be an excellent way to practice lifelong learning. The IEEE sponsors over 2,000 conferences and events worldwide each year, curating innovative content across engineering and technology [6]. Listening to what career professionals have to say and ideas that they have can expose engineers to emerging technologies that might inspire ideas. Conferences are also an excellent networking opportunity, where aspiring engineers can talk directly with industry professionals. I have yet to attend a conference, but will get started by researching relevant conferences through the IEEE conference search portal. 

## Starting independent projects

Projects are an amazing way to spend your free time outside of your careers in order to expand your knowledge. It allows you to dive into areas outside your current expertise, improve your skills without the pressure of work-related consequences, and open doors to new professional connections” [7]. This is a great way to bring an idea of yours to life, which can be an excellent addition to your resume, and keeps you knowledgeable in different areas that may be applicable to your career. I have already started on some side projects, including a drone project that involves wireless networking and communication. 

## Cross disciplinary learning

Cross disciplinary learning allows you to integrate knowledge and perspectives from different fields to solve complex problems. As noted by 180 Engineering, “companies are placing a higher value on candidates who have knowledge that can gap between disciplines” [8]. Learning about other skills, such as public speaking, time management, the stock market, and more can significantly benefit your career, as it shows that you can do more than just technical skills. I have started cross disciplinary learning by volunteering to be MCs in a group setting, and learning the basics in the economic industry. 

## Continuously setting goals for the future

Setting goals is something that everyone should continuously strive for, no matter where they are in life. According to The Ohio State University, “goals can help you stay focused on what you want to accomplish personally, academically, and professionally” [9]. These can help maintain direction, and always gives individuals something to work towards to allow themselves to keep growing. I always set goals for myself, no matter if it's school related or not, and give myself a time frame to complete them by. 

## Reading Books

Reading can expand knowledge, allow individuals to see different perspectives, and spans an infinite amount of topics. It can improve critical thinking skills, teaches social skills, and even strengthens cognitive processes [10]. This directly influences problem solving skills, and teaches an engineer to go beyond what they know and seek new information. Technical readings provide information acquired from industry professionals, which can be very valuable information for an engineer. I have read some readings, such as “Cracking the Code Interview” by Gayle Laakmann McDowel, which has been highly beneficial to my internship interviews.

## Practical Critical Thinking

Practicing critical thinking outside of technical skills or your career is very crucial in keeping your mind healthy and quick. According to Anand College of Engineering, critical thinking in engineering helps problem-solving, decision-making, adaptability, and collaboration [11]. Things like puzzles, projects, and analytical exercises can help engineers to better understand problems, and come up with deeper solutions. I actively practice critical thinking by doing daily puzzles online, ranging from sudoku to word games.

## The Importance of Lifelong Learning

Lifelong learning is very important in the workplace as well as an engineer’s growth in professional development. For one, technology is rapidly evolving. According to a study done by the American Society for Engineering Education (ASEE), the half-life of engineering is just “5 years or less in many technical fields today” [12]. This means that half of the technical knowledge becomes obsolete in only 5 years, which significantly encourages the idea that engineers need to keep learning. Obtaining further education, certificates, and cross disciplinary learning are all ways to make sure engineers stay on top of a rapidly changing curve. 
	
Lifelong learning is also crucial to one’s career growth and financial success. In a study done by the Georgetown University Center of Education, those who engage in continuous learning earn $12,000 more annually on average than those who don’t [13]. The statistics speak for themselves, individuals who allow their minds and skills to continuously grow are more financially successful throughout their careers. Those who are seeking that extra salary boost or even a more outreaching outlook on life are going to benefit heavily from lifelong learning. 
	
According to Kerri Anne Renzulli, a journalist from CNBC, heavy learners are 47% less likely to be stressed at work, 39% more likely to feel productive and successful, and have overall much happier work lives than those who are light learners [14]. Lifelong learning can increase mental capacity, innovation capability, and problem solving effectiveness, which can improve efficiency in a job environment or even one’s everyday life. This reduced stress that comes with lifelong learning stems from confidence in one’s abilities to be able to handle a task when a new challenge arises. 

## In Short

Lifelong learning is a key component to one's continuous personal fulfillment and professional success, especially in a field like engineering where technology is rapidly evolving. By pursuing further education, joining organizations, seeking mentorship, developing cross-disciplinary skills, and much more, individuals gain the ability to adapt throughout their career. Those who commit to continuous growth are far more prepared for challenges, can expand their minds, and are ready to adapt and contribute to a professional environment.

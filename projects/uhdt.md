As a member of the University of Hawaii Drone Technologies team, I had the opportunity to work on the software side of a project focused on developing autonomous unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) designed to detect unexploded ordnance (UXO) in active fields.

In this project, I helped evaluate, test, and ultimately select long-range wireless routing antennas to provide MikroTik routers with a smooth and reliable point-to-point connection. To determine the most effective setup, I performed repeated link tests and closely monitored Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) values, which ranged from approximately -30 dBm to -70 dBm. I also tested different omnidirectional antenna orientations to maximize signal strength and stability as the UAV traveled across different flight paths and distances. 

Throughout the project, I regularly met and communicated with different departments, including the avionics, propulsion, and power sub-teams, to ensure that our networking hardware remained compatible with their systems and processes. Through this experience, I gained hands-on experience with wireless networking, router configuration, hardware integration, and contributing to a large interdisciplinary engineering project.

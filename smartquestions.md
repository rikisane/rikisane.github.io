## Questions: A Crucial Part of One's Journey

In life, asking questions is something that is crucial to help one make progress in whatever they are trying to do. Asking a friend, a professional, or even Google is something that we all have to do someday in our lives. Because of this, asking a good, clear, and "smart" question is very crucial to getting the answer you need. After reading Eric Steven Raymond's [How to Ask Questions the Smart Way](http://www.catb.org/esr/faqs/smart-questions.html), I realized even more how important asking a question is to getting your needs addressed. A smart question provides enough information for someone to understand the problem, preventing the other person from having to solve a mystery or ask more questions before solving the actual issue.


## Asking questions the RIGHT way

To kick things off, let's start with a great example of a question. This comes from a post from 18 years ago, but it still holds up to today's right-question-asking techniques. In this, the developer clearly gets to the point of their problem: they need to merge two Python dictionaries into one expression. On top of that, they provide the EXACT dictionaries that they are looking to merge, as well as the results they are expecting.

![Example of a good Stack Overflow question](../img/goodquestion.png)
[Link to post](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/38987/how-do-i-merge-two-dictionaries-in-a-single-expression-in-python)

As you can see above, the developer does not simply ask, "How do I merge these Python dictionaries?" Instead, they give clear examples of what should happen when the same key appears in both dictionaries. Raymond would be proud of this post, as it gives developers enough information to understand and solve the problem at hand without having to guess.

![Example of a good Stack Overflow question](../img/goodquestion2.png)
[Link to post](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/38987/how-do-i-merge-two-dictionaries-in-a-single-expression-in-python)

A top answer to this question even gave multiple solutions for different versions of Python. Because the poster's question was specific and easy to understand, the answer was exactly what the poster was asking for. On top of all this, the answer received over 9,000 upvotes, meaning it has reached and helped thousands of developers.

## Asking questions the NOT SO RIGHT way

Now that we have covered a great example of a question that gets to the point while providing exactly what details are needed, the post shown below is an example of a not-so-good one.

![Example of a not-so-good Stack Overflow question](../img/badquestion.png)
[Link to post](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/80002300/and-someone-give-me-a-suggestion-to-learn-python-framework)

For starters, the developer explains that they forgot a majority of Flask due to not practicing enough. They then ask which Python framework they should tackle next, as they have only retained about 10–20% of Flask. Grammatically, this question was a little difficult to understand, which definitely brings it down a few points, but on top of this, the question is extremely broad. Details, such as the type of project, what they are interested in doing, why they want to move away from Flask, and more, are missing from this question. This makes it difficult for developers to form an answer without asking for more information. For the cherry on top, this post has a score of negative two with no replies, which speaks for itself.

## Lessons Learned 

After reading Eric Steven Raymond's guide, as well as these two posts, I realized how important a good question is in any aspect of life. The good question gave the developer exactly what they wanted and also helped thousands of others, while the bad question was left in the dust. Looking forward in my engineering journey and in life overall, I will make sure that the questions I ask are clear, include important details, and are as easy as possible for the person I am asking to understand clearly.
